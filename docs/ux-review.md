# Saathi — UX review

**Reviewed:** 31 Aug 2026, against `apps/audit-ui` running via `pnpm --filter @covenant/audit-ui dev:fixtures --port 5301`, driven in Chrome at 1519×784 and 1920×991.
**Method:** every claim below is either **[seen]** (screenshot), **[measured]** (computed style / `getBoundingClientRect` / contrast maths run against the live DOM), or **[source]** (read in `apps/audit-ui/src`). Where I am inferring, I say so.
**Caveat:** two other agents were editing `apps/audit-ui` throughout. The app hot-reloaded and reset session state four times mid-walkthrough. I have excluded every transient build error from the findings below — nothing here is a symptom of someone else's in-flight refactor.

---

## 0. Strategic frame — read this before the critique

One piece of market context changes how you should weigh everything below.

**OpenAI built the thing Saathi does, shipped it, and withdrew it.** Instant Checkout launched 29 Sep 2025; the plan change came ~4–6 Mar 2026 and the discovery-first replacement shipped 24 Mar 2026. OpenAI's own words: *"the initial version of Instant Checkout did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide, so we're allowing merchants to use their own checkout experiences while we focus our efforts on product discovery"* ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-instant-checkout-plan-change-471033); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/24/openai-revamps-shopping-experience-in-chatgpt-after-instant-checkout.html)). Walmart measured it converting **~3× worse** than sending shoppers to retailer sites; ~30 Shopify merchants went live against "over a million" promised ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-agentic-commerce-discover-in-ai-buy-on-site-2026); [Forbes/Goldberg](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasongoldberg/2026/03/10/why-openais-checkout-retreat-spells-trouble-for-its-commerce-strategy/)). Forrester's diagnosis: *"The checkout moment is proving to be the most squirrely to replicate in this channel"* ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/what-it-means-that-the-leader-in-agentic-commerce-just-pulled-back/)).

Critically, **the protocol survived and the UI died.** ACP, delegated payment and the feed spec are all still live and documented ([developers.openai.com/commerce](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/)). What got withdrawn was the *consumer-facing purchase moment*.

This is the best possible news for Saathi and the worst possible news for its current execution.

- The market retreated from the exact problem Saathi is built to solve, and retreated for the exact reasons Saathi's architecture addresses: unverifiable product data, no price provenance, and a purchase moment nobody trusted.
- The demand signal is unambiguous. BigCommerce/Logica (n=3,000, US/UK/AU, Mar–Apr 2026) found **purchase confirmation was the single most critical safeguard in all three markets** ([BigCommerce](https://investors.bigcommerce.com/news-releases/news-release-details/two-thirds-consumers-are-ready-try-agentic-shopping-many-demand)). Gartner (n=322 US, Jan 2026) found willingness to let AI *make* the purchase decision tops out at **11%** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-27-gartner-survey-finds-consumers-want-ai-shopping-help-but-not-ai-purchase-decisions)). Checkout.com found **75% of merchants** call real-time permission revocation critical ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/consumer-demand-for-ai-shopping-is-forming-fast-but-trust-for-agentic-commerce-is-still-catching-up)).
- Saathi's answers to all three — the signed bill, the hold-to-sign gesture, the caps on the Rules page — **are built and are the least visible things in the interface.** The confirmation gesture renders as blank paper (§1.1 #1). The provenance layer renders at 3.68:1 (§1.2 #9).

Two more findings sharpen the point:

**Labels don't work; structure might.** A preregistered Princeton experiment (n=2,012, arXiv [2604.04263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04263), Apr 2026) measured sponsored-product selection at **22.4%** via traditional search, **26.8%** in neutral chat, and **61.2%** when the agent was instructed to persuade. Adding an **explicit sponsorship disclosure moved it only to 55.5%** — recovering roughly 15% of the gap. Fewer than 1 in 5 participants detected any steering. In the same month, Perplexity exited advertising entirely on the stated grounds that even clearly labelled ads make users "doubt the objectivity" of the output ([SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/perplexity-abandons-advertising-ai-search/)), while OpenAI began testing labelled ads ([OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt)). Saathi's *"No sponsored placement"* line plus per-card signed-quote provenance is a **structural** answer where the industry has only a badge. That is the moat. It is currently set in the app's least legible colour.

**The audit trail exists everywhere except the screen.** Google's AP2 produces a signed, non-repudiable intent→cart→payment record — Intent Mandate, Cart Mandate, "what you see is what you pay for" ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol)). **No consumer UI renders it.** Saathi's Inspect drawer is, as far as this research found, the only consumer-facing rendering of an agentic-commerce audit trail that exists. Meanwhile OpenAI's feed spec — the data model behind every ChatGPT product card — has **no freshness or last-updated field at all**, so a card structurally cannot say when its price was last true ([feed spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/feed/)); and ChatGPT's card price is silently the **rank-1 offer** ([Profound teardown](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)). Saathi's *"₹1,299 for 30 of the last 34 days"* is a claim the market leader's data model cannot express.

**So the verdict below should be read as: the strategy is right, the substance is built, and the presentation is actively concealing both.** That is a far better position to be in than the reverse — but it means the fixes in §4 are not polish. They are the difference between having the moat and being able to show it.

## 1. Verdict

The user's summary — "our UI is quite bad" — is half right, and the half that's wrong matters more than the half that's right.

**The content is genuinely ahead of the market.** The provenance-annotated option cards, the "Bill of intent", the six-row Inspect drawer, and the line *"3 fit, listed in the order the agent ranked them. No sponsored placement"* are things no shipping agentic-commerce product does. That is a real moat and it is already built.

**The presentation is undermining it, in three escalating ways.**

### 1.1 Genuinely broken — fix before anyone else sees this

1. **The signing ceremony is invisible.** The hold-to-sign control on the key-creation screen, and the hold-to-seal control on Rules, render as *nothing at all*. `Rosette` is mounted at `stage="idle"`, which sets `stroke-dashoffset: 1` on the only stroked path; the six radial hairlines are `opacity: 0` and the ink bloom is `opacity: 0`; the progress ring circle is `stroke-dasharray: 1px / stroke-dashoffset: 1px`. The button itself is `background: none; border: none`. **[measured]** The result is 96×96px of blank paper. The only affordance is a 10px uppercase caption at 2.44–3.73:1 contrast, positioned `absolute; bottom: -24px`, which escapes its parent's box and lands directly on the next section's `border-top` — so it renders **struck through by a hairline**. **[seen + measured: label occupies y 621–640, `.foot` rule sits at y 630]**

   This is the product's central gesture. The sign-in copy promises *"I will ask you to hold it down, every single time."* Then it shows you nothing to hold. It is the single worst thing in the app.

2. **Voice does not exist in the UI.** `src/voice/**` is 17 files and ~45 KB of real work — Sarvam and Web Speech recognisers and synthesisers, a state machine, mic amplitude metering, a language list. **Not one `.tsx` or `.ts` file outside `voice/` imports any of it.** There is no mic button, no language picker, no listening state anywhere in the running app. **[source: grep for imports returns zero]**

3. **There is no error boundary.** Zero matches for `componentDidCatch` / `getDerivedStateFromError` / `ErrorBoundary` in the entire app. **[source]** Any render throw empties `#root` and leaves a blank cream page with no message and no recovery. I hit this twice. For a product whose pitch is "you can trust what this thing does with your money", silently becoming a blank page is the worst possible failure mode.

4. **The Rules page has no headings at all.** `document.querySelectorAll('h1,h2,h3')` returns `[]`. **[measured]** Settings has no `h1` (only four `h2`s). The Ledger has two `h2`s with empty text content. A screen-reader user cannot navigate any of these pages by structure.

5. **Every money amount is announced without its currency.** `Money.tsx` wraps `₹` in `<span aria-hidden="true">`. **[source + measured on 4 live instances: accessible text is `1,299.00`, not `₹1,299.00`]** In a payments product this is not a nitpick.

6. **The audit drawer doesn't use the audit primitives.** `Hash.tsx` is well built — head+tail truncation, click-to-copy, `title` carrying the full value. The Inspect drawer renders `digest 7e6ed274…` as **plain 12px General Sans text in a non-interactive `<span>` with no `title`**. **[measured]** Not copyable, full value unrecoverable, wrong typeface, and head-only truncation instead of head+tail. Zero `[data-hash]` elements exist on that screen.

7. **The signature designed moment is unreachable.** `frontend-screens.md` §3.2 specifies the T-1 crimson thread break in loving detail — 1.20s, six frames, the barb that stops 6px short. The fixture chat reel is happy-path only, and no route surfaces a blocked transaction beyond a counter chip in the top bar. **[seen: `?scenario=t1-block` changes the chip to "2 blocked" and nothing else on any of the four routes]**

### 1.2 Amateurish — the things that make it read as unfinished

8. **Ragged right edges everywhere.** Section rules and the prose they bracket have different widths, because every text element carries its own independent `ch`-based `max-width` while the rules span the container. On Settings: **rules are 784px wide, prose is capped at 391px** — the rule is exactly twice the text. **[measured]** On the sign-in screen there are four different text measures (367 / 391 / 393 / 402px) and two different rule lengths (540px and ~363px) stacked vertically. **[measured]** This is the single most visible "nobody art-directed this" signal in the app.

9. **Secondary text fails contrast, systematically, and it is exactly the text that carries the product's value.** Measured against `#FAF7F2`:

   | Token | Ratio | Verdict |
   |---|---|---|
   | `--ink-70` | 5.97:1 | passes |
   | `--ink-62` | 4.56:1 | passes, barely |
   | `--ink-55` | **3.73:1** | **fails AA** at every size used |
   | `--ink-40` | **2.44:1** | **fails AA and fails 3:1 non-text** |
   | `--ink-24` (borders) | 1.65:1 | fails 1.4.11 for meaningful boundaries |
   | `--ink-12` (`--rule`) | 1.27:1 | effectively invisible |

   On one chat screen I counted **24 distinct failing text nodes**. **[measured]** They include all seven activity pills, every merchant name, the word **"unverified"** on card C, and **"Quote signed by Sundar Textiles."** The entire provenance and trust layer — the reason this product exists — is rendered in the one colour that fails accessibility.

10. **The composer hides its text field.** Whenever chips, a picker, or a primary action are present, `Composer.tsx` replaces the input with a centred 12px grey link reading "Ask something else" at 3.73:1. **[source + seen]** For most of the flow there is no visible way to type. Every reference product treats the composer as permanently available furniture.

11. **Colour that looks semantic but is positional.** `Composer.module.css` assigns chip dots from five hardcoded hexes (`#e9a23b`, `#1b857e`, `#d95b2b`, `#363499`, `#b23a63`) via `:nth-child(5n+1…5)`. **[source]** None are in `tokens.css`. The ₹1,500 / ₹2,000 / ₹5,000 chips get amber / teal / orange purely by index — three price tiers colour-coded by nothing.

12. **Left-border accent cards.** The evidence panel is `border-left: 2px solid var(--indigo-24)`; the trust summary uses the same device in green. **[measured]** This is the most recognisable AI-generated-UI tell there is, and it's on your two most important components.

13. **Monospace used as a costume.** The design system reserves `--font-data` for "hashes, nonces, amounts". The Ledger sets English sentences in it — including `0/41 quotes broken · 0 manipulation attempts · 3/3 refunds honoured` at 11px, and a literal API signature `tool: recs.suggest(user_id: string, category?: string) -> {...}`. **[measured: 8 non-data mono strings on one page]** Meanwhile the actual hash in the Inspect drawer is set in the body face (see #6). The rule is exactly inverted.

14. **Raw internals on screen.** "11 P3 entries", "4 memories · P3 P3 P2 P1", "bi-temporal ✓", "quote-mismatch 0.4%", "modal band ghosted", "64 ms". **[seen]** Also "1 manipulation attempts" (grammar), and Settings showing NAME "Mehang" while the session is "Demo user". **[seen]**

15. **Small compositional sloppiness.** Two adjacent controls both labelled "New chat". The dock row places a 31px-tall grey link beside a 37px indigo button, misaligned by 3px, left-aligned with ~375px of empty row to the right. **[measured]** The "What the kurta actually sold for" sparkline renders as a flat line and an unattached dotted fragment — it reads as broken. **[seen, both scenarios]**

16. **The app violates four of its own documented rules.** `frontend-screens.md` states them as hard rules; the shipped CSS breaks them:

    | Rule | Stated | Actual |
    |---|---|---|
    | **R2** | exactly one `box-shadow`, only `SigningSheet` | **7 users**: AccountMenu, BillCard, Chat.history, ChatHistory, AddRule, Bench.instrumentColumn **[source]** |
    | **R4** | saffron `#E8740C` never appears in a route | `.shell::after` paints the sunrise gradient across the top of **every** route; computed first stop is `rgb(232,116,12)` **[measured]** |
    | **R6** | no gradients on route surfaces, no backdrop-filter, no floating blobs, no decorative motion | `backdrop-filter: blur(3px)` on the scrim; a **984×984px radial gradient with `filter: blur(90px)` animating `drift` for 46s, infinite alternate** **[measured]** |
    | **R3** | 999px pills for **actions only** | status chips are pills: HealthChip, RangeChip, all ActivityStream pills, TierChip **[source]** |
    | **R8** | every money number carries `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining` | the declaration is present in **20 stylesheets** — and is a **silent no-op** wherever the inherited face is General Sans. See below. |

    The drifting blur is also the most plausible cause of the two 30-second renderer stalls I hit while screenshotting. **[observed twice; causal link inferred, not proven]**

18. **The tabular-figures rule does not work, and cannot work.** I inspected the shipped font binaries with fontTools. **[measured from `src/assets/fonts/*.woff2`]**

    | Face | `tnum` feature | Digit advances | ₹ U+20B9 |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **General Sans** (`--font-body`) | **absent** | **proportional, 306–597** | present (592) |
    | **Fraunces** (`--font-display`) | **absent** | proportional, 512–730 | **absent** |
    | **IBM Plex Mono** (`--font-data`) | n/a — monospaced | **uniform** | **absent** |

    General Sans ships `aalt case ccmp dnom frac kern liga locl mark numr ordn salt sinf ss01 ss02 ss03 subs sups` — and **no `tnum` and no `lnum`**. A font without those features cannot respond to `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining`; the property silently does nothing. Its `1` is roughly half the width of its `0`, so **every money amount rendered in the body face has ragged, non-aligning digits** despite R8 being declared on it. Amounts only actually align where `--font-data` is explicitly applied — and IBM Plex Mono has **no rupee glyph**, so the ₹ beside those aligned digits is served by a fallback face at a mismatched weight. Fraunces has neither.

    This is not a small thing. Independent research across nine Indian fintech products (Razorpay/Blade, Kite, Groww, Jupiter, Fi, PhonePe, Paytm, Slice, CRED) found **not one** of them applying working tabular figures to money — Razorpay's own Inter subset has `tnum` stripped out of GSUB, and its heading face TASA Orbiter has no ₹ either. Saathi wrote the rule down, applied it in twenty places, and landed in exactly the same hole. Fixing it is a font swap, not a redesign — and it would make Saathi the only one in the category that gets it right.

17. **Motion budget blown on the least important screen.** ARCHITECTURE §11 says 150–200ms. `SignIn.module.css` staggers seven children at 0.5s each with delays to 0.54s — **1.04s before the first-run screen finishes assembling**, and the delays are hardcoded `:nth-child(1)`–`(7)`. **[source]**

### 1.3 Fine but unremarkable

The top bar, nav, account menu and Settings are competent and generic. They aren't the problem. They also aren't doing anything for you — the top bar is 40px tall with 12px nav labels at 3.73:1, so the app's chrome is simultaneously cramped and hard to read.

### 1.4 The thing nobody has said out loud

**You have built a desktop app that uses a quarter of the desktop.** Measured widths of the content column against the viewport:

- Rules: **508px of 1920px — 26%** **[measured]**
- Chat: 620px column, inside which the greeting block is **239px wide**, with a **224px dead vertical gap** between it and the composer **[measured]**
- Settings: 784px rules / 391px prose in 1920px
- Ledger: 880px of 1920px — 46%, and the best-composed page in the app, which is not a coincidence

Meanwhile `Chat.module.css` sets `max-width: 820px` on `.chat`, which is dead code because `--dim-calm: 620px` caps the column first. **[measured]** Somebody intended a wider measure and it never took effect.

## 3. Heuristic audit

**Hierarchy.** Inverted on the two screens that matter. On the option cards the decision-relevant facts (30-day price history, merchant reliability, cap compliance) are the *faintest* text in the viewport, descending ink → `--ink-62` → `--ink-55`. **[measured]** The loudest elements on the chat screen are the marigold user-message bubbles at 22% fill — i.e. the content the user already knows. Rules and Settings have no `h1`, so they have no top of hierarchy at all.

**Density.** Two failure modes at once. Components are cramped (40px top bar, 12px nav, 185px cards, 10px section labels) while the page is empty (26–46% width utilisation). The tokens comment says the goal was "smaller components with MORE air around them"; what shipped is smaller components with *unstructured* air around them.

**Rhythm.** The 4px spacing scale is sound and consistently used. What breaks rhythm is the independent `ch` measures — the vertical stack has no shared left-to-right beat, so the eye finds a new right edge every two elements.

**Typography.** The face selection is genuinely good and genuinely distinctive: Fraunces for display, General Sans for body, IBM Plex Mono for data, all self-hosted (R9 honoured). The scale is sensible. Two problems: mono is applied to prose and denied to hashes (§1.2 #13, #6); and the 10px label tier at `--ink-40` (2.44:1) is unreadable — nothing at 10px should carry meaning at that weight.

**Colour.** The palette is right and the discipline is wrong. Indigo (11.36:1), green (5.03:1) and crimson (6.12:1) are all strong. The neutral ramp is where it falls apart: everything from `--ink-55` down fails, and those tokens carry the majority of the interface. Saffron leaks onto every route via the sunrise band, violating R4. The five nth-child chip pigments are outside the token system entirely.

**Motion.** Spec'd beautifully, implemented unevenly. The named easings and the reduced-motion handling are correct. But the sign-in stagger runs 1.04s against a stated 150–200ms budget, and a 984px blurred blob drifts on a 46-second infinite loop behind every screen in direct violation of R6 — the one rule that exists specifically to stop this.

**Affordance clarity.** The worst category. The primary action on two screens is invisible (#1). The text input disappears (#10). Identifiers fail in both directions at once: where `Hash` *is* used it renders as an unlabelled button that looks like text, and where it matters most — the digest in the audit drawer — it isn't used at all, so the one string a user might want to keep is inert (#6). "Amend" links on Rules are the faintest element in their own rows. The three account-menu actions (Settings / Rules / Sign out) have three different visual weights for three peer actions.

**Empty / loading / error states.** Empty: the chat cold start is thin but present; Rules and Settings have no empty states because they're fixture-backed. Loading: `Skeleton.tsx` exists; the activity pills arrive complete rather than progressively, so the agent's work never reads as *happening* — a missed opportunity given that the work is the product. Error: **no error boundary at all** (#3), and the only error surface I could reach is the sign-in screen's "Google sign-in is not configured for this build" — a developer-facing string, in the primary flow, at 3.68:1, above the screen's only button.

**Accessibility.** Focus: the global `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--indigo) }` rule is correct and consistently applied — genuinely good. Keyboard: `HoldToSign` implements Space-to-hold with matching keyup abort, which is thoughtful; but there's no `aria-valuenow`/progress role, so a screen-reader user gets no feedback during the 600ms. Contrast: fails broadly (#9). Structure: missing and empty headings (#4). Currency: stripped from every amount (#5). Reduced motion: correct.

**Responsive.** Effectively absent. **Seven layout media queries across 84 CSS modules; 65 modules have none; zero `min-width` queries.** **[source]** It's desktop-first with a few patches. The option grid jumps 3-up → 1-up at 620px with no 2-up stage, so 621–900px viewports get three ~180px cards. I could not visually verify mobile — the connected browser refused to resize below the window size — so this section is **[source]**-based, not seen.

## 4. The 10 highest-leverage fixes

Ordered by impact per unit of effort.

**1. Make the hold-to-sign control visible at rest.** *(30 min, unblocks the entire product story)*
In `Rosette.tsx`, render the idle state at `strokeDashoffset={0}` with the path at `stroke: var(--ink-24)`, and animate *colour* (ink-24 → indigo) plus the progress ring on hold, instead of drawing the path from nothing. Give `HoldToSign .wrap` a visible resting container: `border: 1px solid var(--ink-24); border-radius: var(--r-full); background: var(--paper-raised)`. Move `.label` from `position: absolute; bottom: -24px` into normal flow (`margin-top: var(--sp-3)`) so it stops colliding with `.foot`'s `border-top`, and raise it to `--ink-70` at `--t-body-s-size` (12px). Add `role="progressbar"` + `aria-valuenow` to the ring. Apply identically to the Rules hold-to-seal trigger.

**2. Retire `--ink-55` and `--ink-40` for text.** *(1 hour, fixes 24 failures at once)*
Set `--ink-55: color-mix(in oklab, var(--ink) 64%, transparent)` (≈4.7:1) and `--ink-40: color-mix(in oklab, var(--ink) 55%, transparent)` for any text use; keep a separate `--ink-hairline: 12%` for rules only. Then raise the 10px label tier: `--t-label-size: 11px` and label colour `--ink-70`. Highest-value single change in the document — it repairs the provenance layer, the activity pills, "unverified", and the nav in one pass.

**3. One measure per page, set on the container.** *(1–2 hours, kills the "unfinished" read)*
Delete the per-element `max-width: 42ch / 46ch / 52ch` declarations in `SignIn.module.css`, `Settings.module.css` and siblings. Set a single `--measure: 62ch` on the page container and let rules, headings and prose share the same right edge. Raise `--dim-calm` from 620px to 720px and delete the dead `max-width: 820px` on `.chat`.

**4. Give the composer back its text field.** *(1 hour)*
In `Composer.tsx`, always render `.field`. Show chips *above* it rather than instead of it, and delete the "Ask something else" escape hatch entirely. Right-align `primary` in `.row` (`margin-left: auto`) so Confirm sits at the end of the row rather than floating mid-left, and match its height to the field (37px).

**5. Add an error boundary.** *(30 min)*
Wrap `<Screen>` in `App.tsx` in a boundary that renders, on the same paper, in the assistant's voice: what failed, that nothing was charged, and a "Start again" button. A money product that can blank to nothing has no business shipping.

**6. Collapse the activity pills to one expandable row, and stop saying "Everything checks out".** *(2–3 hours, biggest perceived-quality jump)*
Replace the 7-pill wrap with a single summary line — `✓ Checked 3 merchants, 14 listings · quarantined 4 claims` — expanding to the full list on click, following the pattern ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini all converged on. Promote the security-critical step: quarantine events get `--crimson` marks and stay visible when collapsed; routine steps hide. Keep the Summary/Normal/Verbose control but move it inline with the summary row, not floated right. Monzo's rule is the one to apply: *"providing clarity isn't about being explicit in every detail but only when it is valuable"* — a field that is always present has stopped carrying information, which is precisely what seven identical green ticks are.
Separately, kill the headline **"Everything checks out"** and promote the sub-line that already exists. Chrome removed the padlock because 89% of users inflated a narrow technical claim into "this is safe"; an unqualified "everything" invites exactly that. Lead with the named claim — **"Quote signed by Sundar Textiles · memory digest verified · 8 of 8 checks passed"** — and drop the "64 ms". Replace the eight anonymous tick circles with nothing, or with a three-state row (passed / failed / not applicable) in Vanta's manner, so the marks can express something other than unanimous success.

**7. Use the `Hash` primitive on every ID; keep middle truncation; name what you copied.** *(1 hour)*
Route the Inspect drawer's digest, the ledger head/chain hashes and the signing-key thumbprint through `<Hash>`. Keep **middle** truncation — for a hash, every character is equally distinguishing, so the task is *comparison*, not reading, and middle truncation maximises the independent positions a human can eyeball (PatternFly is the only system that documents this position and requires ≥4 visible characters either side). Go from 4+4 to **6+6** (`7e6ed2…8f41c3`): git abbreviates to 7 and auto-lengthens to preserve uniqueness, so 4 is below the honest floor for a growing ledger. Add `aria-label={`Copy ${label}`}`, a `border-bottom: 1px dotted var(--ink-24)` so it reads as interactive, and replace the 200ms flash with an `aria-live` confirmation that **names the value** — `"Memory digest copied"`, per Cloudscape, not `"Copied!"`.
*Note the live tension:* Cloudscape sanctions inline copy in key-value pairs; Primer argues untruncated is the most accessible option and that tooltips are the wrong recovery mechanism; Stripe exposes **no monospace token and no code component at all** across its entire third-party surface. There is no consensus to appeal to — but "always-visible copy affordance, labelled, middle-truncated, full value in `title`" satisfies every one of them except Primer's purist position.

**8. Make money actually work: real tabular figures, a visible rupee, one format per context.** *(2 hours; the highest-value hour in this list is the font swap)*
Three parts, in order:
- **Get a body face with `tnum`.** General Sans has no tabular figures, so R8 is inoperable everywhere it's declared. Either swap `--font-body` for a grotesque that ships `tnum` (Söhne, which Groww licenses, does; so does upstream Inter — though ARCHITECTURE.md rules Inter out as an AI-default tell, and that objection stands), or keep General Sans for prose and set **every money amount in `--font-data`**, which is monospaced and therefore tabular by construction. If you take the second route you must also fix the ₹: **IBM Plex Mono has no U+20B9**, so pair it with an explicit fallback that does, or draw the amounts as `--font-data` digits with the symbol in General Sans (which has ₹ at a 592-unit advance, near-identical to its `0` at 593 — which is why `.symbol { width: 1ch }` currently works by luck rather than design).
- **Un-hide the rupee.** Delete `aria-hidden="true"` from `Money.tsx`'s symbol span; every amount is currently announced without its currency.
- **Make the format rule legible.** Use `rupeesRounded` (₹1,299) everywhere a *product price* appears — cards, prose, chips — and reserve two-decimal `paise` for the bill, the ledger and budget remainders. Right now the same amount appears as ₹1,299.00 and ₹1,299 in one viewport and the governing rule is invisible to the reader.

Do **not** reach for `notation: 'compact'` while doing this. It is unsafe for money: `₹99,999` renders as `₹1L`, `₹99,99,999` as `₹1Cr`, and `₹9,99,999` and `₹10,00,000` both as `₹10L`. Razorpay's own Blade `humanize` mode inherits all of these. Saathi's `rupeesRounded` uses `maximumFractionDigits: 0` rather than compact notation, which is the correct choice — keep it.

**9. Honour R6 and R2 — delete the blob, keep the band.** *(30 min, likely fixes the renderer stalls)*
Remove `.glow` / `.glowWarm` and the 46s `drift` animation from `App.module.css` entirely. Keep the 3px sunrise band (it's a good gesture) but move it behind an explicit exception in the spec, or desaturate its first stop off `#E8740C` so R4 holds. Replace the six unauthorised `box-shadow`s with `border: var(--rule)` + `--paper-raised`, per R2.

**10. Give Rules and Settings a heading, and fix the mono/prose inversion.** *(1 hour)*
Add `<h1>` to `Covenant.tsx` ("The lines I cannot cross") and `Settings.tsx` ("What I hold for you"), styled `--t-display-l-size` Fraunces, matching the Ledger — which is the only page that gets this right and, not coincidentally, the best-composed page in the app. Fill or remove the Ledger's two empty `<h2>`s. Then move every English sentence on the Ledger out of `--font-data` into `--font-body` — "0/41 quotes broken · 0 manipulation attempts" is a sentence, not a dataset — and delete the raw `tool: recs.suggest(...)` signature or move it behind "For auditors".
On the digests, follow Cloudscape rather than instinct: mono is sanctioned for ID strings but should be set **smaller than body** (their scale puts inline code at 12px against 14px body) and comes with an explicit "avoid overusing monospaced typefaces". Stripe goes further and ships no mono token at all. So: `--font-data` at `--t-data-s-size` for the digest itself, body face for everything around it. The inversion is the bug; more mono is not the fix.

*Deferred but flagged:* voice is unwired (§1.1 #2) — either wire it or cut the directory before review; the T-1 crimson break is unreachable (§1.1 #7) — it's the best thing in the spec and nobody can see it; responsive is effectively unbuilt (7 media queries / 84 files); and `--font-accent: "Anek Devanagari"` is declared in `tokens.css`, **used by nothing, with no `@font-face` behind it and no Indic glyph in any shipped binary** — so ARCHITECTURE §11's "bilingual accents" do not exist. Either load Anek and use it (the greeting is a natural home: "नमस्ते" beside "Namaste.") or delete the token. Note that Sarvam has the identical gap, and Razorpay's Blade ships Cyrillic, Greek and Vietnamese subsets but no Indic script at all — so shipping actual Devanagari would put Saathi ahead of both.

## 5. What to keep

These are genuinely distinctive and must survive any redesign. Several are better than anything shipping.

**Before the list, the finding that should govern it.** Across Sigstore/Rekor, Certificate Transparency, AWS CloudTrail and Tailscale's tailnet lock, the research found the same thing every time: **the transparency infrastructure is mature and the consumer UI does not exist.** CloudTrail's hash chain is not visualised anywhere in the AWS console — enabling validation "does not validate the integrity of the files"; the only user-facing surface is `aws cloudtrail validate-logs` printing text. Sigstore's search UI is a dropdown and a text field. There is **no worked example anywhere of a well-designed consumer-facing hash-chain or inclusion-proof view.** Google's AP2 defines a complete signed intent→cart→payment record that no consumer UI renders at all.

Saathi's kolam ledger line and Inspect drawer are, on this evidence, the first serious attempt at that view. That is not a nice-to-have feature. **It is the thing to build the product around**, and it is currently rendered at 3.73:1 behind an invisible button.

- **The provenance-annotated option cards.** "signed quote" vs "unverified" on the card itself, plus the evidence panel's cap-compliance / 30-day price history / merchant-reliability triple. No shipping agentic-commerce product attaches an audit trail to a product card. Fix the contrast and the size; do not touch the idea.
- **"3 fit, listed in the order the agent ranked them. No sponsored placement."** One sentence that does what an entire trust-and-safety page usually fails to do. Keep the wording exactly.
- **The Bill of intent.** Dashed rules, the mark centred on the tear line, tabular right-aligned amounts, `Total` in semibold. It reads as a paper receipt and it is the most finished thing in the app. It also happens to match a 2002 IBM patent's three-part specification of a digital receipt almost exactly — a description "in a format understandable by humans", tamper-proof evidence that "preferably is hidden from display" because it "will be unintelligible to humans", and a **verification prompt** ([US7694332B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7694332B2/en)). That is the whole design thesis in one sentence: the claim is the headline, the hash is the footnote, the verification is a button. Keep the receipt; add the third part. (One fix: "Your ceiling ₹2,000.00" sits in the line-item list as though it were a charge — move it to an annotation under the total.)
- **The Inspect drawer's six rows.** "Your ask / What it relied on / Cart built / Checks / Payment rail / Outcome" — plain-language labels, one summary line each, expandable. This independently reproduces the pattern the whole industry converged on in 2026, and it satisfies the constraints the literature imposes: two disclosure levels, not three (NN/g: "designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability"); domain-language section names rather than payload keys (Okta's Actor/Client/Event/Request/Target); and a panel that is supportive rather than required (Cloudscape caps these at 3–4). Strip the raw tier codes ("P3 P3 P2 P1") and the "64 ms", add absolute UTC timestamps, and it is genuinely best-in-class — because there is nothing else in the class.
- **The `ProductPlate` reasoning.** *"Inventing a photo for a thing we have never seen is the same confident fiction the write gate exists to refuse."* That is the right principle. The right *implementation* is: show the merchant's real image when a signed quote carries one, fall back to the deterministic weave when it doesn't — and say which you're showing.
- **The kolam language** — the Truchet weave plates, the rosette seal, the background tile at 0.018 opacity. It is culturally specific without being a tourist poster, which is genuinely hard. The rosette needs to be *visible*, not removed.
- **The money engine — the arithmetic half.** Integer paise end to end, one formatter, `Intl.NumberFormat("en-IN")` so grouping is correctly ₹1,23,456.00, and `rupeesRounded` implemented with `maximumFractionDigits: 0` rather than `notation: 'compact'`. That last choice looks minor and isn't: compact notation renders ₹99,999 as "₹1L" and ₹99,99,999 as "₹1Cr", and Razorpay's own Blade `humanize` mode inherits every one of those hazards. Keep the formatter exactly as it is — then fix the font that renders its output (§1.2 #18).
- **The consent screen itself.** Our bill shows item, ceiling, total, merchant and who signed the quote. Razorpay's own agentic-payments hero mockup shows a purchase authorised with "Yes, proceed." and **no amount, no payee, no cap**. Measured against RBI's April 2026 e-mandate framework and NPCI's confirmation-screen requirements, Saathi's consent moment is more compliant than the market's illustration of the future. That is a pitch line, and it is true.
- **The `Hash` primitive's design** (head+tail, click-to-copy, `title` with the full value) — it just needs to be used.
- **The sign-in "what signing in cannot do" clause.** Leading with the limits of the credential, before the button, in the assistant's voice. That is a real trust move.
- **The Ledger page's composition and its section titles** — "Which merchants keep their word", "Prove it: replay from zero". Conversational headings over an audit log is the correct instinct; make the other three pages match it.
- **Focus rings and reduced-motion handling.** Both correct, both consistently applied.

Step lists are often post-hoc rationalisations; NN/g recommends sources and stated limits over confident step narration. Our pills describe *verified gateway events*, not model reasoning, which is a defensible answer to this critique — but the distinction should be visible in the UI.
- Perplexity's *"Completed N steps"* → plain-language substeps, and ChatGPT's *"Thought for 5s"* → Activity panel. Both keep the trace one row tall until asked.

**Composer**
- [ChatGPT's shipped CSS](https://gist.github.com/alexchexes/d2ff0b9137aa3ac9de8b0448138125ce) — `--thread-content-max-width: 40rem` shared by composer *and* prose; `max-height: 50dvh` autogrow cap. Directly relevant to fixes #3 and #4.
- Claude's composer: model and effort legible before the first keystroke — the closest analogue to showing *your* caps and key before the first ask.

**Cold start — four deliberate answers**
- [Gemini's 2026 redesign removed suggestion chips](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-app-android-redesign-3668250/) in favour of a larger greeting ("Hi [name], what's on your mind?"), while [ChatGPT made chips personalised](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai/chatgpt) in Aug 2026. Same year, opposite bets — worth choosing consciously rather than defaulting.

**Inline objects and the side-panel question**
- [OpenAI removed Canvas in May 2026](https://www.krasa.ai/news/openai-gpt-5-5-instant-writing-coding-blocks-canvas-removed-may-2026) for inline writing/code blocks. Our Inspect drawer is a deliberate bet against this trend; Claude's artifact split-pane is the surviving precedent for it.
- ChatGPT's shopping carousel — [anatomy and placement](https://www.darkroomagency.com/observatory/how-chatgpt-shopping-transforms-online-purchasing). Note it is the one element allowed to break the reading measure.

**Streaming**
- [Upstash, *Smooth Streaming*](https://upstash.com/blog/smooth-streaming) and [Streamdown's animation docs](https://streamdown.ai/docs/animation) — buffer network chunks and render at a constant rate; word-level fade-in at 150ms. Relevant if you make the activity pills arrive progressively (fix #6).
- [Streaming UI patterns that don't break](https://thepromptbench.com/ai-product-ux/streaming-ui-patterns-that-dont-break/) — the anchored/drifted/re-anchored scroll model, with the ~100px threshold.

**Measure and readability**
- Claude constrains by `max-w-[75ch]`, tied to font metrics rather than a pixel guess — the model for fix #3.

**Verification badges — what to copy and what to fear**
- [GitHub commit signature verification](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/managing-commit-signature-verification/about-commit-signature-verification) — a **four-state** ladder: Verified / Partially verified / Unverified / *no badge at all*. The best idea in the whole gallery: **decide what the unremarkable case looks like and make it look like nothing**, because badging every ordinary object "Unverified" burns the signal. Also: verification records persist "even if signing keys are rotated, revoked", with a `verified_at` field — separating "was valid when checked" from "is valid now", which naive verification UIs conflate.
- [Chrome removed the padlock in v117](https://www.thesslstore.com/blog/google-to-replace-the-padlock-icon-in-chrome-version-117/) — 89% misread it; 11% understood it precisely. Underlying research: [Porter Felt et al., *Rethinking Connection Security Indicators*, SOUPS 2016](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2016/technical-sessions/presentation/porter-felt). Read this before shipping any green tick.
- [Stripe's Badge component](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/components/badge.md?app-sdk-version=9) — six states that separate **outcome** (Positive/Negative, "no action required") from **demand for action** (Warning/Urgent). A "signed" badge belongs in the outcome family, not the alert family.
- [Cloudscape status indicators](https://cloudscape.design/components/status-indicator/index.html.md) — four colours only, no amber tier, and "Never rely on color alone".

**Making a hash human**
- [Signal's safety numbers](https://signal.org/blog/safety-number-updates/) — the deepest published reasoning anywhere. Renamed from "fingerprint" because that metaphor "doesn't carry well" (people hear police records). 60 base-10 digits in 12 groups of 5, chosen for localizability, visual/audible distinguishability, and because it is **half the comparison size of the previous hex format**. Verification moved from per-user to **per-conversation** because that's what users actually want to verify. And "Mark as verified" is deliberately a *user assertion*, not a system claim — a distinction Saathi should make explicitly.
- [1Password's Secret Key](https://agilebits.github.io/security-design/apsk.html) — `A3-ASWWYB-798JRYLJVD4-23DC2-86TVM-H43EB`. The grouping is **irregular (2-6-11-5-5-5) and semantic before it is ergonomic**: the first two groups are exactly the non-secret version and account ID. Anyone who copies "chunk into blocks of N" loses the better half of the idea.
- [PGP word list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGP_word_list) and SSH randomart — two more encodings of the same bytes. The lesson: the design question is never "how do I display this hash" but **"over which channel will two humans compare it"** — screen, print, voice, or glance.
- Real truncation lengths in production: git 7 (auto-lengthened for uniqueness), Tailscale 8, GPG long ID 16, Okta 20, CloudTrail fingerprint 32, CloudTrail hash 64, SSH fingerprint full. [PatternFly](https://www.patternfly.org/ux-writing/truncation/) is the only system documenting middle truncation and a ≥4-character floor; [Primer](https://primer.style/accessibility/patterns/truncation/) argues untruncated is most accessible and that tooltips are the wrong recovery; [Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/components/key-value-pairs/index.html.md) forbids truncation in key-value pairs outright. No consensus exists — say which one you're following and why.
- [Stripe on object IDs](https://dev.to/4thzoa/designing-apis-for-humans-object-ids-3o5a) — underscores were chosen over colons **so that double-clicking selects the whole ID**. A separator choice that determines whether a copy button is necessary.

**The technical drawer, done calmly**
- [Stripe Workbench](https://docs.stripe.com/workbench/overview) — the developer console as a **resizable pane with a zero state**: maximise / minimise to taskbar / collapse to a single icon, opened from anywhere with `~`, and Auto-inspect **off by default**. The page never carries developer furniture.
- [AWS CloudTrail event detail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail/latest/userguide/view-cloudtrail-events-console.html) — raw JSON lives at the **bottom of the page**, gated by scroll rather than a toggle, so the user is never asked whether they're the sort of person who wants JSON. Its [validation CLI output](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail/latest/userguide/cloudtrail-log-file-validation-cli.html) is the model for honest verification reporting: `22/23 digest files valid, 1/23 INVALID`, success silent in non-verbose mode, and separate "Results requested" / "Results found" lines because the attestable range differs from the requested one.
- [Datadog Audit Trail](https://docs.datadoghq.com/account_management/audit_trail/) — "Inspect Changes (Diff)" renders config changes as green/red JSON. Rather than gating the payload, it makes the payload the answer to the question users actually have, and lets colour do the reading.
- [Brex transaction drawer](https://www.brex.com/support/brex-dashboard-accounting-page) — audit log placed **third**, after the two things a normal user came for.
- [Cloudscape secondary panels](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/secondary-panels/index.html.md) — panels must be "supportive but not essential"; cap at 3–4.
- [Blowfish wallet design reference](https://docs.blowfish.xyz/docs/wallet-integration-guide) — `humanReadableDiff` shipped as a **sibling API field** to `rawInfo`. The plain-language claim comes from the verifier, not the frontend.

**Consent and disclosure**
- [Plaid's Data Transparency Messaging](https://plaid.com/docs/link/data-transparency-messaging-migration-guide/) — stopped showing product names, started showing **data scopes** ("Transactions", "Account and balance info"). Disclosure sits **above the Continue button**; "why" is one tap down behind "Learn more". Note the asymmetry: the consent-relevant fact is always visible, the justification is secondary — most products invert this. The most trust-critical copy is the copy customers **cannot** edit.
- [Plaid Portal](https://plaid.com/core-exchange/docs/consent-management/) — clicking a data type expands to show **when it was last accessed**, turning a static permission grant into a living log. Directly applicable to Saathi's Rules page.
- [Mozilla's security-warning redesign](https://blog.mozilla.org/ux/2019/03/designing-better-security-warnings/) — 22–50% fewer bypass attempts. Half of participants never opened "Advanced", so everything actionable stayed on the main screen. Read the participant quote about vagueness; it is the argument against hiding too much.
- [GOV.UK Details](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/) — "Do not use the details component to hide information that the majority of your users will need." Plus the research finding that some users won't click a disclosure because they fear navigating away.
- [NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) — never more than two levels.

**Money legibility and the purchase moment**
- [Wise's transfer status tracker](https://wise.com/help/articles/2452305/how-do-i-check-my-transfers-status) — three named stages, each answering "where is my money and who is holding it": *"Your money's being processed"* → *"Money received"* → *"Transfer Sent"*, with an explicit handoff sentence. Compare to a bare `SETTLED` badge.
- [Monzo on merchant enrichment](https://monzo.com/blog/2018/02/15/android-crowdsourcing-merchant-data) — raw merchant data is "an indecipherable string of letters and numbers"; the fix is name, location, logo. The anti-Etherscan position, in a bank. And their activity-feed principle: *"providing clarity isn't about being explicit in every detail but only when it is valuable."*
- [Mercury's transaction redesign](https://mercury.com/blog/new-transactions) — the amount moved **next to the merchant name**; timelines showing initiation and posting; MT103 as a **download**, not an inline render.
- [Baymard on perceived payment security](https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form) — 19% recent abandonment on card-trust grounds; encapsulating payment fields raises perceived security; and the finding that fabricated seals outperform real ones, which together with the Chrome padlock proves that badges are read as vibes and the claim must live in a sentence beside them.
- [Cloudscape timestamps](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/general/timestamps/index.html.md) — three formats with stated triggers; never abbreviate months or units. Note the tension: relative time is friendlier but uncitable in an incident report, which is why CloudTrail defaults to absolute UTC against Cloudscape's own recommendation.

**Etherscan — the failure to study**
- [Blockscout on why block explorers fail users](https://www.blog.blockscout.com/block-explorer-ai/) — the diagnosis is not that Etherscan shows hex; it's that **the hash is the page title**. The most prominent element is the least meaningful one to the reader. Their fix is a reordering, not a removal, and they name the target explicitly: it "mirrors traditional receipt formats — clear outcome information with detailed backup data accessible if required."

**Indian fintech — the house we're building in**
- [Razorpay Blade](https://github.com/razorpay/blade) (MIT) — the actual product design system, and worth reading rather than guessing at. Type: **Inter** body / **TASA Orbiter** headings / Menlo code. Spacing `0,2,4,8,12,16,20,24,32,40,48,56`; radii `0,2,4,8,12,16,20,24,9999`; weights 400/500/600/700 only; motion `80/160/200/280/360/480/640/960ms`; colour authored in **HSLA**, not hex, with ramps named azure/emerald/crimson/cider/sapphire. Note for anyone matching Razorpay's brand: **the navy is `#072654`, not `#02042B`**, and there are three live blues — `#3395FF` (logo), `#305EFF` (marketing), `#1364F1` (product). Its `Amount` component fades the ₹ and the decimals (`isAffixSubtle`) — a good idea we could steal for the bill.
- [razorpay.com/agentic-payments](https://razorpay.com/agentic-payments/) — read this **as a warning, not a model**. The hero mockup depicts a UPI Reserve Pay consent with no amount, no payee and no cap. Saathi's bill sheet is the corrective.
- [Razorpay + NPCI agentic payments on Claude](https://razorpay.com/blog/agentic-payments-and-npci/) (India AI Impact Summit, 20 Feb 2026) — UPI Reserve Pay, one-time consent-based per-merchant spending limits, instant revocation, piloting with Zomato/Swiggy/Zepto. This is the rail Saathi's Settings page already describes. NPCI's [Unified Agent Protocol](https://clearingpost.com/insights/npci-unified-agent-protocol-agentic-upi/) — register, verify and authorise agents on UPI — still awaits RBI approval.
- [Zerodha Kite](https://zerodha.tech/) — the density benchmark, and the only Indian fintech whose public design writing is about **restraint and user protection** rather than brand: pre-trade **nudges** for illiquid stocks and far-from-LTP orders, plain-language rewriting of CNC/MIS/NRML, user-selectable fonts. Their stated aim — a typeface chosen because Kite "is filled with numbers" — is, ironically, not implemented at the OpenType layer either.
- [D91 Labs, *Tricked by Design*](https://d91labs.substack.com/p/tricked-by-design-deceptive-patterns) — an audit of nine Indian fintech apps finding seven families of deceptive pattern. The rigorous India-specific UX study, and it is about ethics, not aesthetics.

**Regulation that is really design spec**
- [RBI Digital Payments E-mandate Framework, 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13374&Mode=0) (21 Apr 2026 — **this consolidated and repealed all eight predecessors**, so most blog guidance you'll find is stale). §4 mandate validity + customer-set maximum + withdraw at any time; §6 pre-debit notice ≥24h carrying merchant name, amount, date/time, mandate reference and reason; **§7 post-transaction notice must now include grievance-redressal details**; §8 AFA thresholds ₹15,000 general / ₹1,00,000 for insurance, mutual funds and card bills.
- **NPCI UPI Procedural Guidelines** — the confirmation screen is specified: *"amount, transaction reference details, address to which payment is being made, clear indication if the merchant address is whitelisted, and the payment confirmation details post payment."* Two ideas worth stealing wholesale: **Verified Name is pulled from bank CBS and cannot be spoofed, while the VPA can** — so a UI that leads with the handle inverts the trust hierarchy; and the **"address verified" indicator** for whitelisted merchants, which is precisely Saathi's signed-quote chip by another name.
- [RBI TAT/compensation circular](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693&Mode=0) — failed UPI credits auto-reverse at T+1 (P2P) / T+5 (P2M) with **₹100/day compensation credited *suo moto***. A pending state that says "check with your bank" is hiding a guarantee the user is already owed.
- [Dev et al., *From Cash to Cashless*, CHI 2024 LBW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09937) — ~75% of surveyed UPI users report **increased spending**, attributed to the payment's "intangible nature". Empirical support for the whole Saathi thesis: making money legible is a behavioural intervention, not a styling choice.

**Indic design without the kitsch**
- [Gopalan, *Symmetry Classification and Enumeration of Square-Tile Sikku Kolams*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14134) — kolam as a formal grammar: every dot individually encircled, crossings discrete, **no loose ends**, six tiles, eight point groups, and a parity theorem (Drop + Fan tiles must be even) you can literally lint generative patterns against. If the kolam language is going to be load-bearing, this is the spec.
- [Meher Varma, *Labels*](https://fiftytwo.in/story/labels/) — the essential essay, because it defeats the obvious answer. Her terms are **"kitsch minimal"** and **"truck art clean"**: going minimal to avoid kitsch is itself the trap, and produces brands that "make India appear exotic to Indians". Read before any restraint-based redesign.
- [November on pluralism in Indian design](https://walkerart.org/magazine/pluralism-indian-design-november-shiva-nallaperumal-juhi-vishnani/) — *"the slip into mimicking kitsch only happens when the practitioner is not informed"*, and Indian-ness "cannot be a single set of visual cues or gimmicks".
- [PhonePe's identity](https://thehardcopy.co/phonepes-brand-identity-case-study/) — the structural exemplar: **the rounded descender of the Devanagari पे becomes the terminal rule for an icon set that contains no Devanagari**. Their [Petris illustration system](https://www.phonepe.com/blog/design/petris-unifying-phonepes-illustrations-for-a-stronger-brand-identity/) specifies 9-head adult / 6-head child proportions and posture rules. Structure, not ornament.
- [Sarvam's brand page](https://www.sarvam.ai/brand) — our stated benchmark, and worth correcting the record on: the system is a **blue-to-orange spectrum** on near-white `#FCFCFC`, not saffron→indigo on warm cream; indigo `#212191` is right. The strong move is the cusped gateway arch used as a **negative-space mask** over gradients and documentary photography, never as an outlined ornament — plus non-standard variable weights (425, 525) that only a variable font makes possible. Fraunces is variable; we use 400 everywhere.
- [Kulkarni, *Issues with Devanagari Display Type*, Typoday 2013](https://www.typoday.in/2013/spk_papers13/sarang-kulkarni-typographyday2013.pdf) and [Devanagari Typography 101](https://www.alphabettes.org/devanagari-typography-101-a-guide-for-typesetting-with-latin/) — if bilingual type ever ships: **Devanagari has no x-height**; match Latin x-height to the *shirorekha*, and never letterspace Devanagari (it breaks the headline). [Ek Type's Anek](https://github.com/EkType/Anek) is ten scripts authored separately to cohabit — the right model, and the font `--font-accent` already names but never loads.
- The anti-kitsch method, from the 2024 Indian packaging cohort: every good example references a **specific, locatable source** — Athangudi tiles, Rani Bagh, Channapatna toys, Indian Railways liveries — and never "India". Specificity is the mechanism. Saathi's Tamil kolam and Truchet weave already qualify.
- ⚠️ [colorarchive.org's "India traditional palette"](https://colorarchive.org/regions/india/) claims documented pigment provenance; three of its eight colours are **Google's 2014 Material Design defaults** with Indian nouns attached and two more are the national flag. Do not source a pigment palette from it. For real pigment data use Harvard's [Mapping Color in History](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-01729-4) — which also found that a Jaipur master's *neel* is Prussian blue, not indigo. Tradition is materially syncretic; naming pigments as heritage without checking is branding, not research.

## Appendix — measurement notes

Screenshots were captured in the connected Chrome at a 1519×784 render of a viewport that oscillated between 1519×784 and 1920×991 as the window settled; geometry figures state which. Font claims (§1.2 #18) come from reading the shipped `.woff2` binaries in `apps/audit-ui/src/assets/fonts/` with fontTools — cmap coverage for U+20B9 and U+0915, the GSUB/GPOS feature tag list, and `hmtx` digit advances normalised to a 1000-unit em. A font whose feature list contains no `tnum` cannot respond to `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, so that is a property of the binary rather than of the CSS. Contrast ratios were computed in-page by compositing each element's `color` over its nearest opaque ancestor background on a 1×1 canvas and applying the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula — this correctly resolves the `color-mix(in oklab, …)` alpha tokens, which cannot be read off the token file directly. Two screenshot attempts failed with 30-second CDP timeouts; the DOM remained responsive to JavaScript throughout, which is why the drifting blurred layer is the leading suspect but not a proven cause.
